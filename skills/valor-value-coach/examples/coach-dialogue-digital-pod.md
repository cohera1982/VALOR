# Example — Value Coach: multi-benefit, value concentrated where adoption is hardest (digital proof of delivery, Foods)

**User:** I want to build digital proof of delivery. Today drivers get a goods received note or a stamp on a thermal-paper delivery note. The stamp fades, so disputes are hard to reconcile, and we manually scan and verify 800 delivery notes a day.

**Coach:** So: stamps on thermal paper fade, disputes are hard to settle, and 800 notes a day are scanned by hand. Got it. Here's my first guess:

> When **digital proof of delivery lets customers confirm receipt on the driver's device**, **[who does what differently?]**, so every delivery has proof that doesn't fade, which means we win disputes we lose today, get paid faster, and stop scanning 800 notes a day.

What's the biggest prize? 1. Disputes we lose today · 2. Getting paid faster · 3. Scanning effort · 4. All about equally

**User:** 4

**Coach:** We'll keep all three and size each. Faster payment and fewer lost disputes partly overlap, so we'll make sure no invoice counts twice. Who has to do something differently? (pick any) 1. Drivers capture proof · 2. Customers sign digitally · 3. Credit control uses it · 4. Back office stops scanning

**User:** all four

**Coach:** One of those depends on people outside the company: your customers' receiving staff. Will they accept a digital sign-off? 1. Most will · 2. Big retailers won't · 3. Mixed by channel · 4. Not sure yet

**User:** 3

**Coach:** Where key accounts insist on their own note, the driver can photograph it with time and location: still proof that doesn't fade. What do we know about disputes today? (pick any) 1. We track credit notes · 2. Mostly modern trade · 3. Payments held up · 4. A hunch so far

**User:** 1, 2, 3

**Coach:** That's the crucial link, and there's a tension: most disputes come from the channel least likely to sign on the device. So the dispute benefit depends on key accounts accepting photo-of-note proof. I'll put that on the card.

*(One card, three quick questions: time freed · top two watch-outs · "Does this capture anything about people, like signatures or photos, that needs careful handling?")*

**User:** Redeploy · picks all four watch-outs · Not sure

**Coach:** You picked all four, which is fair. The two that most threaten the value go on the card; slower drops is a cost trade-off, so it goes under trade-offs, and no signal becomes a design need.

> When digital proof of delivery lets **drivers capture a signature, or a photo of the customer's own goods received note, at every drop**, credit control settles disputes from proof that doesn't fade, which means **fewer credit notes conceded and faster payment on disputed invoices**.
